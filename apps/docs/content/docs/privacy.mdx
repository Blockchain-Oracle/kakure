## The promise

> The public sees nothing beyond what any shielded Zcash transaction shows. The operator sees what was bet, never
> who bet it.

Two layers make that true:

- **The protocol.** A fully shielded, same-pool payment hides its sender, recipient and amount. Your bet is one.
- **Kakure's design.** A shielded payment carries no sender address, and the memo carries no identity: only the
  market, side, ticket size, a random nonce and your payout address.

Kakure does **not** claim anonymity of positions from the operator, cryptographically verifiable odds, or trustless
custody.

## Who sees what

| Who | Learns | Never learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anyone watching the chain | That shielded transactions happened, with their timing, fee and shape | Who paid whom, how much, the memo, or that a transaction is a bet |
| The operator | Each bet's amount, side, arrival time and payout address | Who you are or which wallet paid. Clients never contact the operator; it only publishes signed files |
| Other bettors and the public | Each market's ticket count and ticket hashes, published only in groups of at least 3 new tickets; each side's totals once both sides have 3; the final totals and the result | Any single bet: its side, its size or when it arrived |
| This website's host | Your IP address, when you visited and which pages you opened | Your bets, sides, payout address, network or the markets you look at. The QR is built in your browser, market ids stay in the `#fragment`, and every visitor downloads the same files for both networks |
| Kakure's price relay | Your IP, and when a tab showing prices is open | Which prices you watch: it streams every symbol to everyone, and your browser sends it nothing |
| Your wallet's server | Your IP, when your wallet syncs and broadcasts, and which transactions it fetches (bets and payouts included), unless your wallet uses Tor. With Zodl or Zingo that's your wallet's own server; the Kakure app syncs with zec.rocks and sends over Nym, so zec.rocks sees its IP when it syncs | The memo or the amount |
| An exchange where you cash out | The amount you deposit | Which bet it came from: every same-size winning ticket pays the same |

## How the design gets there

- **No accounts, no bet API.** The site is static. The app and site never call the operator; the operator pushes
  signed files to storage, and every visitor downloads the same ones.
- **The same requests for everyone.** Every visit loads the same `latest.json`, bundle and address list, for both
  networks, on the same clock, whichever one you use; market ids live in the URL fragment, which browsers never
  send. On its first visit the site's service worker downloads every script and the address checker, the same set
  for everyone, and asset logos are drawn from the page itself, so opening a market or the bet
  sheet asks the host for nothing about it. The Proof page downloads all of a batch's resolution evidence at once,
  never one market's.
- **A fresh market address per bet**, picked in your browser from the signed list, so bets don't share an address.
- **Fixed ticket sizes**, so amounts don't fingerprint a bet, and every same-size winner gets the same payout.
- **Published in groups, never one by one.** A market's ticket count, its list of ticket hashes and its totals
  change only when at least 3 new tickets have arrived, and the odds stay hidden until each side has 3. If fewer
  than 3 tickets are still waiting when the market's cutoff block is final, they're refunded in full and never
  published. A signed batch goes out every 10 minutes whether or not anything changed.
- **Payouts on a timer, never right after a bet.** Each payout is split into canonical 1, 2 or 5 × 10^k notes, each
  sent as its own transaction at a random time 2 to 6 hours after resolution, with the outgoing viewing key
  discarded. Payout transaction ids are never published.
- **Prices through Kakure's relay.** Your browser never connects to an exchange, so no exchange sees your IP next
  to Kakure.

## What Kakure can't hide

1. The operator sees every bet's amount, side and payout address, though not who placed it.
2. Odds are reports signed by the operator, not cryptographic proofs.
3. Custody and resolution are trusted: there are no smart contracts on Zcash.
4. Every published change, the final totals included, covers at least 3 new tickets. If all the tickets in a group
   took the same side, that group's side and total show (not whose tickets they are), and someone who places most
   of a group themselves can work out the rest (the n−1 attack).
5. If fewer than 3 tickets are still waiting to be listed when a market's cutoff block is final, they're refunded in
   full and never published. A bet that arrives last, alone, doesn't play.
6. Miners set block timestamps within consensus limits, so the cutoff block has some latitude.
7. Each signed list holds 1,024 market addresses and your browser picks one at random, so two bets in the same batch
   can share one: with n bets in a batch the chance is at most n(n−1)/2,048, under 5% for 10 bets. Only the operator
   can see it. Each batch, used addresses are replaced together with random unused ones, so a new list doesn't show
   which were paid.
8. Your own funding history and your wallet's server are outside Kakure's control.
9. The operator reads the chain from public light-wallet servers (zec.rocks) over Tor, so it relies on them to serve
   the chain honestly.
10. While few people use Kakure, the set of bettors you hide among is small.
11. The published market addresses could be decrypted by a future break of today's cryptography (harvest now,
    decrypt later).
12. Stock markets will settle on two keyed sources (Pyth and Alpaca) that you can't re-fetch yourself.

To hide your IP from the website's host, open Kakure in Tor Browser.
