## The promise

**The prices are public. You aren't.** One shielded deposit becomes private chips, blind-signed so the mint can't tell
which deposit they came from. Every request travels through an Oblivious HTTP relay: the relay sees your IP but only
encrypted bytes, and the operator sees the trade but never your IP. There is no account and no sign-in.

## Who sees what

| Who | Learns | Never learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anyone watching the chain | That shielded deposits and payouts happened, with their timing, fee and shape | Amounts, memos, who sent them, or which trades they paid for |
| This site and its relay (one Cloudflare worker) | Your IP, when you visit and trade, which pages you open, and which network you trade on | What's inside a request (sealed to the operator's published key); the asset or Window you look at |
| The operator | Each deposit's amount; each trade's Window, side, stake and time; each withdrawal's address and amount | Your IP; which deposit paid for which trade; who you are |
| Everyone else | The price formula, volatility, spread and caps; signed prices and outcomes; the chip keys; what the house owes, hourly and rounded up; volume per Window in whole groups of three trades | Any single trade |
| Kakure's arcade service | Your nickname, the key your device made for it, your runs (game, score, inputs, the day) and your duels (calls once both are locked in, result, the day) | Your IP or any header of yours, your wallet, chips or trades; Practice and Lucky never reach it |
| Kakure's price relay | Your IP, when a tab showing prices is open, and which candle interval your chart shows and how far back | Which asset you watch |
| Your wallet's server | Your IP, when your wallet syncs and broadcasts, and which transactions it fetches, unless your wallet uses Tor | The memo or the amount |
| An exchange where you cash out | The amounts it receives | Which trades they came from |

## How the design gets there

- **Chips, blind-signed.** Your deposit buys chips: bearer tokens the mint signs without seeing them (Chaumian e-cash,
  Cashu's BDHKE on secp256k1). Each signature comes with a DLEQ proof against the one published set of chip keys, so
  the mint can't mark a chip to recognise it later, and your app refuses proceeds that aren't paid in the standard
  split. When you trade, you spend chips the mint has never seen unblinded, so it can't tell which deposit paid.
- **Oblivious HTTP for every request.** Deposits, trades, closes, redemptions and withdrawals are each sealed to the
  operator's gateway key (RFC 9458, HPKE with X25519, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-128-GCM). The key is in the signed bundle,
  so everyone encrypts to the same one, and it changes every hour; a device that slept through a change gets the new
  key from the next bundle and sends the same request once more.
  The relay builds a fresh request carrying only its content type, so no header of yours, and not your IP, reaches
  the operator. Its Worker keeps no logs. Requests and answers are padded to a few fixed sizes, so their length says
  nothing about the call or how many chips it carries.
- **The same files for everyone.** Every visit loads the same signed files for both networks, on the same minute
  clock, whichever one you use. The asset and Window you look at live in the URL fragment (`/trade#BTC/5m`), which
  browsers never send.
- **Prices through Kakure's relay.** Your browser never connects to an exchange. The relay streams every symbol to
  every visitor and serves every symbol's candles in one response, so it can't tell which one you chart.
- **Standard sizes and your own timing.** Stakes snap to the chip ladder (1, 2 or 5 × 10^k), so a stake never
  encodes your balance and is paid in exact chips without change. Each position is redeemed at its own random time,
  20 seconds to 3 minutes after its Window, and calls go one at a time.
- **Volume in groups of three.** A Window's trade count and volume count only whole groups of three trades, never
  one trade's size, and a partial close never shows in it.
- **Withdrawals split and spread.** A withdrawal leaves as standard 1, 2 or 5 × 10^k notes, each its own transaction
  at its own random time, so an exchange that receives them can't match them to a trade.
- **The house's books, hourly.** The signed bundle states the chips outstanding and the most the open positions
  could pay, taken once an hour and rounded up to the per-side cap, so no single trade, deposit or withdrawal shows
  in the change.

## What Kakure can't hide

1. **Timing.** A trade placed right after a deposit, or a withdrawal right after a win, can be matched by time.
   Chips come in standard amounts, stakes in standard sizes, and redeems and withdrawals at random times; spreading
   your own activity out does the rest.
1. **Linking trades.** Each trade spends chips the mint can't trace, but the operator still sees every trade's
   Window, side, stake and time, so amounts and timing can hint that two trades share an owner, more so while few
   people trade.
1. **Kakure runs both the relay and the operator.** Oblivious HTTP is built for two separate parties, so keeping
   your IP apart from your trades rests on Kakure not combining the two. Using Tor keeps your IP from both.
1. **Custody and the house's solvency are trusted.** The hourly liability statement is the check.
1. **Position certificates aren't blinded,** so the operator links a position's open to its close or redemption.
   That's the same anonymous trade, so it reveals nothing new.
1. **The n−1 case.** Volume moves in steps of at least three trades; someone who places two trades of a group can
   work out the third's stake.
1. **The per-side cap.** A trade the cap refuses tells whoever placed it which quarter of the cap that side of the
   Window has reached.
1. **Signed reports.** Prices and outcomes are signed by the operator, but each print is an exchange's public
   1-minute candle, so anyone can fetch them again and recompute.
1. **The chain source.** The operator reads the chain from public light-wallet servers over Tor, so it relies on
   them to serve it honestly.
1. **A small crowd.** While few people use Kakure, the crowd you hide among is small.
1. **Harvest now, decrypt later.** Oblivious HTTP and Zcash's note encryption use elliptic-curve key exchange, so
   traffic and transactions recorded today could be read after a future break of that cryptography.
1. **The gateway key.** The operator's Oblivious HTTP key changes every hour and the old one is deleted ten minutes
   later (RFC 9458 §6.6). If the operator were ever broken into, requests recorded on the way in the last hour or so
   could be read, never older ones. In those ten minutes, a request's one-byte key id also shows whether its device had
   fetched the new key yet.
1. **What the operator keeps.** Deposit records and spent-chip fingerprints stay for good: they stop a deposit
   minting twice or a chip spending twice. A withdrawal's address and amount go a week after it's paid, and an address
   already paid can't be used again.
1. **The arcade isn't sealed.** The site sees your arcade calls (nickname and scores) with your IP, though it keeps no
   logs; the arcade service never sees the IP. A nickname is public, so pick one that doesn't name you.
1. **This browser.** It keeps your trade settings (stake size, trail, starred markets), your arcade key, nickname and
   runs and Practice's tally, and its history keeps the pages you opened. Lucky's draws, like your chips and
   positions, are kept encrypted in its vault. Forget this device clears the app's part.
1. **Your side.** Your funding history and your wallet's server are outside Kakure's control.
